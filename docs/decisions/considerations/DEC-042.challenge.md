# DEC-042 — Independent challenge

Independent challenge for DEC-042, per `docs/DIRECTIVE_PROTOCOL.md` § 1a. Its
only job is to argue against the memo's recommendation. It re-runs the memo's
searches itself. A challenge that only agrees is treated as missing.

## Strongest case for a different option

**Option 2 (record the owner answers, build no checks) is the better call, on
this repo's own evidence.**

The memo's Option 1 lands two new checks and records two owner answers in one
Tier 2 DEC. That bundles a mechanical, near-zero-risk bookkeeping act (recording
the owner's answers) with a gate change, and the gate change is the weak half:

1. **The checks have nothing to catch yet.** `bakeoff` slots do not exist —
   DEC-041 says plainly "No bake-off was run", and no issue in the live queue
   carries the `bakeoff` label with a slot body. `needs:human` is created and
   the repo has no open `status:blocked-needs-input` issue without it (the
   live-queue scan is clean). So both new checks are written against a
   population of size zero. The repo's gate discipline is explicit that a check
   earns its place by catching something; these catch nothing observable today.
2. **They are already covered by prose.** § 3 step 2 and § 4 both state the
   requirement in the workflow the agents read. Adding a scanner for a rule with
   no violations is the "plausible artifact from an unchecked process" in
   reverse: a check whose PASS cannot be distinguished from "nothing to check".
3. **Cost is not zero.** Two checks, two fixture pairs, a docstring, a fixture
   JSON edit, and a `trailer`-style special case are all surface area the next
   agent must keep green, for rules nobody has yet broken.

If the two owner answers are the real content of DEC-042, Option 2 delivers
them at Tier 0 cost and defers the checks until a bake-off or a blocker
actually exists to check.

## Conflicts the memo missed

- **C10 — the memo's own "Why now" is the argument against it.** § 2 says
  "waiting a week changes nothing technically". A Tier 2 decision with a
  24-hour floor is justified by irreversibility, not by "the owner asked". If
  waiting changes nothing, the memo has not shown why this cannot wait for a
  real bake-off to exist and be checked against.
- **C11 — self-referential ratification.** DEC-042 ratifies itself against a
  gate it is simultaneously changing. The Tier 2 floor is waived in the same
  breath as adding checks that count waivers. That is the DEC-041 weakness
  (recorded, single shared account) repeated one day later, and it means the
  "pilot works if overrides read as exceptions" acceptance now has two
  overrides in two days — evidence against, not for, the premise.
- **C12 — tier inflation cuts both ways.** § 1a says "when unsure, pick the
  higher one", and the memo now claims Tier 2. But the same section says the
  process "scales with how expensive the decision is to undo". An advisory
  check inside an existing gate is cheap to undo (the memo's own § 5 says so).
  The memo cites the rule to raise the tier while its Reversibility section
  argues the tier down. One of the two is wrong.

## Cheapest experiment that would settle it

Instead of deciding: **record the two owner answers as a Tier 0 addendum to
DEC-041's existing entry** (no new DEC), and **defer both checks** until the
first bake-off parent or the first unlabeled blocker exists. Then add the check
*with* its first real fixture — the live case — which is strictly stronger than
a synthetic fixture and costs the same. Settles the question in one run, and the
answer is observable: if no bake-off and no unlabeled blocker appear within the
pilot's N directives, the checks were never needed and Option 2 was right.

## Where the challenge agrees

- The two owner answers should be recorded; leaving §12 `NEEDS:` open is worse
  than either option.
- The `flow.open_total` answer (leave the definition, record the artefact) is
  correct and is not what this challenge disputes.
- If Option 1 is taken anyway, the checks must stay advisory and fixture-proven,
  which the memo already requires.
